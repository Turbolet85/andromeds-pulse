## Goal
The console engine gains its first way in besides telemetry: a door inside its own process. A caller on the
engine's host connects to a Unix-domain socket under the data dir, sends the same JSON-RPC 2.0 lines the stdio
sidecar speaks, and is answered by the sidecar's nine tools reading the stores the running engine holds, not a
database of the door's own (intent F6; scope, "What the chunk builds"). Only the engine's owner is answered: the
socket sits in an owner-only directory, its mode is 0600, and the door reads each peer's account from the kernel
and refuses any other, root included (P-126). Every call is bounded in size and in time, every call and every
refusal leaves an allowlisted record with no content in it, and the one write, resolving an incident, is named as
a write and can be switched off.

**The cut (the operator's answer, inputs#I3).** This chunk builds the door and removes nothing. The sidecar, its
double gate, the window's side of it, the `mcp-test` job and the tests that spawn the binary leave in a removal
entry the operator mints at this chunk's wrap, directly after this one. For that one interval the engine has two
ways in besides telemetry: the door (console engine only) and the stdio sidecar (a second process reading the
corpus on disk). What forbids a third is in "Constraints & rejected approaches".

**The surface is PROVISIONAL.** The endpoint, the owner check, the reach and the workflow step that runs a client
as a second account are the operator's answers of 2026-10-10 (the pc overseer, inputs#I3), a boundary widening
under the playbook's `Boundary widening` pattern. The founder's own word is owed on the surface itself. Nothing

4. **The door** — new `pulse-app/src/door.rs`, declared in `pulse-app/src/lib.rs`. It names no window framework
   and holds no store of its own. What it does:
   - **Endpoint.** The directory `run/door/` under the data dir, created mode 0700 before anything is bound, and
     the socket `run/door/sock` inside it, mode 0600. The path is resolved as `write_pid_file` resolves `run/`:
     canonicalized and refused if it leaves the data dir. A path longer than a socket address holds (108 bytes,
     `libc-0.2.186/src/unix/linux_like/mod.rs:204`) leaves the door unstarted. A socket file left by an earlier
     run is replaced only when it is a socket and nothing accepts on it; a path a live listener holds, or one that
     is not a socket, leaves the door unstarted. In each unstarted case the engine goes on, as it does for a
     rejected receiver port.
   - **Owner check.** On every accepted connection the peer's account is read from the kernel
     (`tokio::net::UnixStream::peer_cred`, tokio 1.52.1) and compared with the engine's own effective account. A
     different account is refused: the connection is closed with no byte written. The comparison is one function
     with no other caller-identity input.
   - **Calls.** One JSON-RPC 2.0 request per line, one answer line per request that carries an `id`; the methods
     are `initialize`, `tools/list`, `tools/call` and `ping`, through the envelope functions of `jsonrpc.rs`.
     `tools/call` goes through step 1's form with the engine's own handles: a read connection taken with
     `viz::query::read_connection` from the engine's buffer connection, the engine's `CorpusWriter`, and the
     engine's `incident_workspace_key`. No `Corpus::open`, no `open_in_memory`, no read of `run/workspace-key`.
   - **Bounds, set here.** A request line of at most 65 536 bytes; an answer of at most 1 048 576 bytes; a call
     of at most 5 s; a connection idle for 30 s is closed; at most 8 connections at once. An over-long line is
     answered with `-32600` and the connection closed. An answer over its bound is not truncated: the caller gets
     error `-32003` naming the bound. A call over its time bound answers `-32002`, and the door interrupts its own
     read connection (the form at `crates/triage/src/baseline/sql.rs:462-482`). A ninth connection is closed at
     accept.
   - **The write switch.** `mark_incident_resolved` is the one write. With the switch off the door refuses it
     with `-32001` before the dispatcher is called; `tools/list` still names it. The tools crate's error type is
     untouched.
   - **Records, through `tracing` to the engine's one file sink, nothing to stdout or stderr.** `app.boot.door`,
     once per boot of a program that seats the door: `state` (`listening` | `path_too_long` | `in_use` |
     `occupied` | `bind_failed`) and `resolve_write` (`on` | `off` | `rejected`); INFO when listening with the
     switch not rejected, WARN otherwise. `door.call`, once per request that got an answer: `method`, `tool` (one
     of the nine names, or `none`), `outcome` (`ok` | `tool_error` | `invalid_arguments` | `unknown_tool` |
     `unknown_method` | `parse_error` | `write_off` | `timed_out` | `answer_too_large`), `write` (bool),
     `result_type`, `result_count`, `answer_bytes`, `duration_ms`; INFO for `ok`, WARN otherwise. `door.refused`,
     once per connection or line refused before an answer: `reason` (`other_account` | `too_many_connections` |
     `request_too_large` | `idle`) and `peer` (`owner` | `other`); WARN. No record carries an argument, an answer,
     a path, an account id or an error's text, and none is written at ERROR.

## Constraints & rejected approaches

  equality in `integration_console_engine.rs`. No engine part wired outside `engine_boot::start`:
  `unit_engine_boot_seam.rs`. No command word added: the console command line stays `run` | `version`
  (`pulse-app/tests/unit_console_commands.rs:33`). No TauRPC procedure and no capability grant added: the drift
  and staged gates. The scope guard reads no file outside the sixteen.
- A socket in `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` or the temp dir — rejected: a fourth product-written place outside the data
  dir, absent from every harness child environment (inputs#I3, answer 1).
- A loopback TCP port — rejected: every local account and browser tab can dial it, so owner-only would need a
  credential this entry's requirement leaves to a later entry (inputs#I3, answer 1).
- A door in the window program — rejected: it has no reader there and the window leaves in Epoch 2.
- A bridge command of the engine for stdio callers — not built: no command word is added here (inputs#I3,
  answer 1). See the note on stdio agents below.
- A variable that turns the door off or moves its endpoint — not built: one variable is added, the write switch.
- Moving a harness verb onto the door — not here: the readiness and status verdicts are member-pinned
  contracts, and their owners are `working-route.md:45` and `:47` (scope, closed at P3).
- Changing the sidecar, its records or `dispatch_tool`'s signature — do not: the sidecar stands unchanged for
  the interval, apart from the `write` member its tool list gains.
- An error variant in the tools crate for the switched-off write — do not: the door refuses before the
  dispatcher is called.
- Logging the socket's path, a peer's account id or an error's text — do not.

## Implementation notes
- **How an agent that speaks over stdio reaches the door (inputs#I3, answer 1).** During this interval the
  sidecar is still the stdio way in. The door itself speaks over a socket, so a stdio client needs a stream
  relay between its stdio and `run/door/sock`; this project ships none, and whether a general-purpose relay is
  installed on this host was not measured. The first-class way for an agent on the developer's machine belongs
  to `Door reachable from another host` (`working-route.md:100`), which names admitted parties and where a
  party's credential lives on the developer's machine. No route entry owns a same-host stdio relay: once the
  removal entry lands, a same-host stdio agent has only a general-purpose relay. That gap is put to the
  operator on the review card for the entry minted at this wrap.
- **The form an outside reader depends on (inputs#I1 item 3), to be written into architecture §Standard
  Contracts at the wrap.** Connect: a Unix-domain stream socket at `run/door/sock` under the engine's data dir,
  as the engine's own account. Call: one JSON-RPC 2.0 object per line, UTF-8, at most 65 536 bytes;
  `tools/call` carries `params.name` and `params.arguments`. Answer: one line, `result` as the tool returns it
  today, at most 1 048 576 bytes. Error: the JSON-RPC error object with `-32700`, `-32600`, `-32601`, `-32602`,
  `-32603`, or the door's own `-32001` (the write is switched off), `-32002` (timed out), `-32003` (answer too
  large). A caller of another account sees the connection closed with no byte.
