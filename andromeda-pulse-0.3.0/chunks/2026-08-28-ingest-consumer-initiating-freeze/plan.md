Give the wedge a **named cause** and turn a one-observation mystery into a **re-runnable scenario with a
verdict**. Research (`research.md`) falsified the working entry's causal premise on all three clauses and
reconstructed the real timeline from the preserved 131 MB corpus: `17:20:50` is the end of injector run 1

2187. Tier1 `service_went_silent` cadence triggers had begun at `17:23:21`, **~55 s before** that onset. So
load at freeze onset was heavy, the storm **preceded** the stall, and the cue→cadence runaway is a **trigger
candidate**, not merely the amplifier the entry describes.

That reframes the non-reproducibility too. The predecessor's matched 15-minute leg ran `--sustained`, which
never stops emitting — so no producer gap, no silence storm, no runaway, and a healthy 47,898 rows. It
reproduced the *stated* preconditions faithfully; the stated preconditions were the wrong ones. The
injector's arg-less default is **finite** (`TOTAL_BATCHES = 600` × `BATCH_INTERVAL = 500 ms` ≈ 300 s, then
exits — `crates/ingest/examples/inject_demo.rs:133-135`), so consecutive default runs create the gap
naturally, which is how the original wedge arose.

The chunk therefore **verifies and pins** rather than repairs (operator-selected at P4 from a three-option
fork). `2026-08-26-cadence-runaway-blocking-pool` already bounded the amplifier at cue emission
(`CueLatch`, 60 s refractory — `crates/triage/src/cue/emitter.rs:28`). If the wedge's real preconditions no
longer stall at HEAD, that IS the attribution: the bound closes the initiating freeze, and the chunk says so
