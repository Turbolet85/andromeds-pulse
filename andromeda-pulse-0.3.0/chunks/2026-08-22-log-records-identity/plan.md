## Goal

Two log records emitted by one service, in the same nanosecond tick, at the same severity — a burst of `INFO`
lines, the ordinary case — collide on `log_records`' primary key `(ts_unix_nano, resource_hash,
severity_number)`. This chunk makes both survive ingestion.

**The chunk is MEASURE-FIRST, and the measurement comes due here for a reason.** Research found that
behavioural primary-key enforcement has **never been verified anywhere in this codebase**:
`crates/buffer/src/schema.rs:311-318` records that a duplicate-INSERT probe "was observed to hang in
libduckdb-sys 1.10502 on this build," so the PK tests assert schema *metadata* and explicitly defer
behavioural enforcement to "the appender path." That deferral is this chunk's first task. Until it is
settled, the defect is a well-grounded hypothesis, not a fact — and if the Appender turns out not to enforce
the constraint, both records already land, the finding dies, and the chunk closes as a premise correction
(scope sanctions this outcome explicitly; precedent at `2026-08-15-tier-1-incident-path-investigation` and
`2026-08-14-fingerprint-feed-capture-repair`).

Research also corrected the defect's shape. The route entry called it a silent drop of the *second record*;
structurally it is neither silent nor per-record. `append_table_traced(...)?` propagates out of
`dispatch_batch` (`crates/buffer/src/consumer.rs:143`) and `run_consumer` logs it at ERROR on
`duckdb.append` with a `reject_reason` (`:78-83`) — while `record_rows_appended` is skipped and the
broadcast emit suppressed. So a single colliding pair costs **the entire Logs batch**, and because ingest
counts log records at the receiver *before* the buffer (`crates/ingest/src/grpc.rs:225`,
`http.rs:357`), the ingest counter keeps climbing while zero rows land — the same
counter-divergence class as `app.boot.buffer.degraded` (per obs-plan §6). Per security-plan §Threat Model
Summary → Attack surface, that also means one local producer can cost a well-behaved producer's whole batch.

The fix (operator-selected 2026-08-22) adds a **monotonic sequence column** `seq BIGINT NOT NULL`, allocated
from a buffer-global counter, extending the key to `(ts_unix_nano, resource_hash, severity_number, seq)`.
The rejected alternatives and why are in §Constraints & rejected approaches — in particular a per-batch
ordinal mirroring `span_events.event_index` was considered and rejected because it closes only the
intra-batch half of the class.

## Constraints & rejected approaches

- **Per-batch ordinal (`record_index`, mirroring `span_events.event_index`) — REJECTED.** `event_index` is
  safe only because its key carries `(trace_id, span_id)`, a unique parent that scopes the ordinal.
  `log_records` has no unique parent, so a per-batch reset leaves two same-nanosecond records arriving in
  different export batches both at index 0 — still colliding, still costing a whole batch. It closes the
  intra-batch half of the class and leaves the rest.
- **Widening the key with `body` / `trace_id` / `span_id` — REJECTED**, from two independent directions:
  `body` is scrubbed before persistence (`extract_log_body` → `scrub_otlp_field`, `appender.rs:290-307`), so
  identity would depend on redaction output and two records differing only in a redacted credential would
  collide *after* scrubbing; and untraced log records carry empty `trace_id`/`span_id`, so those columns do
  not separate the colliding case at all.
- **Dropping the primary key — REJECTED** (operator decision 2026-08-22). It would close the class, but makes
  `log_records` the only one of 8 tables with no identity and renders true duplicate re-sends permanently
  undetectable.
- **Do NOT give `seq` a column DEFAULT** to avoid touching the three fixture INSERTs. A default would let a
  fixture row take an unintended ordinal, contradicting the ordinal semantics the acceptance criteria assert,
  and would hide exactly the miss the grep check exists to catch. Supply it explicitly at every site.
- **Do NOT plan around a const-vs-`SCHEMA_DDL` hand-sync hazard** — `ddl_constants_match_concatenated_schema`
  (`schema.rs:380-396`) already closes it; the two `schema.rs` sites are one table's two representations, not
  two tables. Do not add a sync step and do not report it as a finding.
- **Do NOT build the span-side drop-observability counter.** A span rejected on `(trace_id, span_id)` reading
  as a dead feed belongs to the "Diagnostics un-muting + harness-truth sweep" entry. If this chunk's work
  suggests a shared counter shape, name it in the report for that entry to consume — do not build its half.
- **Do NOT touch `log_records.severity_text` scrubbing** — one of the five uncovered columns owned by the
  "Ingestion scrub coverage" entry (tail position 2).
- **Do NOT extend the fix to `metrics_points`** — its PK `(metric_name, ts_unix_nano, resource_hash)` omits
  the point's attributes and carries the identical defect, but it is a separate entry's subject. Say in the
  report whether this fix generalizes, so that entry can be minted with evidence.

## Implementation notes

- **Order sensitivity:** step 1 gates step 3 onward. Do not write the DDL change before the measurement
  records the failure mode — a fix landed on an unverified premise is exactly what the measure-first posture
  exists to prevent, and the falsification outcome is a legitimate deliverable.
- **The hang hazard is the plan's main trap.** The intuitive probe (`INSERT` the same key twice) is the one
  documented to wedge on this build. Everything behavioural goes through the Appender.
- **Sequence allocation is per batch, not per record** — one `fetch_add` reserving a block, consistent with
  `record_feed_counts` / `record_redactions`, which exist precisely to keep the per-record loop atomic-free.

  `buffer.tick`, the `viz` `SELECT_LOGS` column list, or the MCP response shape. Adding it to any of those
  turns an internal counter into an observable with a three-site obs amendment and an allowlist leaf.
