## Goal
The predecessor chose the L4 model. This chunk ships it: the founder's pick, the unsloth `gemma-4-E4B-it-Q4_K_M.gguf`
(sha256 `85a896a0…ab87`). The product's L4 subprocess now runs it with the sampling its authors publish
(`--temp 1.0 --top-p 0.95 --top-k 64 --min-p 0`) and thinking off (`-rea off`, kept). Its output is constrained by a
committed GBNF passed through `--grammar-file`, in place of today's `--json-schema-file`. On b9305 the json-schema form is
prefilled with the template's generation prompt and kills gemma4 at sampler init. A user grammar is never prefilled
(re-derived at b9305 source, research.md §Mechanism re-derivation). The GBNF is the b9305 converter's output for the
shipped schema, and a test performs that conversion and pins the equality (founder ruling 1, inputs#I1). The test runs
the converter, vendored at b9305 under its MIT notice.

The founder also raised the L4 GPU SLO (ruling 2). Research found the latency grader cannot fail at HEAD: the latency
sample carries no `hardware_profile`, the grader drops every sample without one, and its p99 is a floor index. So this
chunk makes the grader gradable and raises the gpu-primary budget from 5000 ms to **10000 ms (p99, nearest-rank)**. The
live CUDA run with the pick is then graded by the grader itself, and a mutation shows the grader failing over budget
(overseer, founder-delegated, P4).

Per architecture §Established Decisions [LLM Inference Runtime], security-plan §Input Validation (the L4 argv row) +
§Security Anti-Patterns → Code Patterns, obs-plan §8 + §10 + §11, test-plan §1 + §3. No llama.cpp bump, no prompt or
schema change, no TauRPC or capability change.
