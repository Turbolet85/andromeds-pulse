## Color Moods

### Mood 1: "Planetarium Control Projection Room"

**Physical space:** A modern planetarium's projection control room at night — a dark, temperature-controlled chamber where operators navigate constellation maps and dome lighting systems through networked displays. Red and amber safelights illuminate touchscreen panels and key controls, allowing operators to maintain dark adaptation while monitoring real-time star positions and dome mechanics. The space feels enclosed, purposeful, with the hum of cooling systems and the glow of dimmed LED indicators.

**Color palette (derived from this space):**
- Deep Space Black (#0F0F0F) — the planetarium dome interior and surrounding enclosure, dominant background surface
- Observatory Amber (#D4922F) — safelight gels used on control panel accent keys to preserve night vision while enabling precise interaction
- Signal Red (#C92A2A) — emergency/alert indicators and critical status markers on equipment panels, visible without breaking dark adaptation
- Stellar Indigo (#3B5BDB) — constellation projection mapping colors (Linear-inspired minimal accent), used for active traces and service highlights

**Sensory anchor:** The space breathes with quiet purpose — dim enough to see projection calibration, bright enough to work. Red and amber lighting creates visual hierarchy without eye strain during hours of focused monitoring. Sound is minimal: cooling systems, quiet keyboard clicks, the soft hum of LED backlighting — a contemplative, tech-enabled darkness.

### Mood 2: "Telescope Eyepiece Observation Platform at Night"

**Physical space:** An observatory's elevated eyepiece platform in darkness, where an astronomer peers through a telescope control interface mounted next to the ocular. Warm red safelights shine on a mounted control panel and hand-written observation logs. The secondary monitor (a tablet or small display) shows catalogued star positions and real-time tracking data. The physical space is open to the night sky, cold, minimalist — just the scope, the platform, and a single red reading light.

**Color palette (derived from this space):**
- Midnight Navy (#1C1E2D) — the night sky and surrounding darkness around the observation platform
- Warm Red Safelight (#E74C3C) — red light sources used to preserve night vision while reading logs and adjusting controls (documented astrophotography standard)
- Deep Steel (#3A4D5C) — control panel frame and structural components (aluminum/steel), visual grounding for interface
- Pale Star White (#F5F7FA) — constellation labels, critical data text visible against the dark platform, monospace coordinate displays (Linear-inspired typographic clarity)

**Sensory anchor:** Cold night air, the mechanical precision of the telescope tracking, and the focused quiet of solitary observation. Red light feels intimate and ancient — it's how astronomers have observed for generations. The interface is purely functional: text and controls only, no ornament, built for precision work with numb fingers and fatigued eyes.

### Mood 3: "NASA Deep Space Mission Control Station (Modern Artemis Design)"

**Physical space:** A workstation within NASA's modern Artemis Mission Control room — a secondary monitor console staffed during real-time spacecraft tracking operations. The walls are dimly lit with blue accents (symbolizing Earth), the carpeting depicts lunar mineral crystalline patterns in gray, blue, and burgundy. Each station has task-specific directional lighting (20-100 lux for night ops), LED panel backlighting in cool tones, and red alert indicators for anomaly detection. The space feels coordinated, purposeful, grid-like — designed for marathon observation sessions.

**Color palette (derived from this space):**
- Deep Control Gray (#1A1D24) — mission control console base and surrounding interior, dark enough for eye comfort during 8+ hour shifts
- Alert Burgundy (#8B2E3B) — anomaly and alert states, visible but not jarring (derived from NASA's lunar anorthite carpet pattern colors)
- Earth Blue (#4A90E2) — accent lighting (representing Earth), used for non-critical but important state indicators (mission-phase markers, system status overview)
- Status White-Blue (#E8EEF7) — critical display text and data streams, slightly cool-tinted for long viewing comfort and circadian rhythm alignment (NASA lighting standards)

**Sensory anchor:** The space hums with purposeful tension — multiple displays, coordinated teams, the steady glow of status panels. Burgundy alerts command attention without panic; blue accents create a through-line connecting operators to the mission's larger context. The lighting is engineered to sustain focus over hours, preventing eye fatigue and respecting biological needs.

## Recommended

**Recommended mood:** Mood 1 — "Planetarium Control Projection Room"

**Reasoning:** This mood best extends the Q1 "Ambient constellation — luminous, patient, emergence-driven" personality with a concrete operational space that mirrors andromeda-pulse's core function: real-time pattern recognition in a dark, tech-enabled environment. The planetarium control room directly instantiates the brief's "Service constellation" signature element (animated dot field scaled from tray icon to full dashboard) — just as operators navigate constellations emerging from the dome, developers navigate service topologies emerging from the dark dashboard. The amber safelight palette (observatory standard since the 1950s) grounds the design in physical reality rather than abstract "cool palette" trends; red alerts preserve the operator's dark adaptation without theatrical drama, aligning with Q2's Linear philosophy (motion-as-data, restraint over gradient). Raycast's command-palette invisibility parallels the planetarium's "silence until summoned" operational mode — the app is ambient presence until interaction, at which point the red/amber interface emerges. Dark background (#0F0F0F) is sustainable for 24/7 presence on a developer's monitor (the widget's core use case), while the amber accent provides warm, focused interaction points — rejecting the cold blue-on-black sterility of Datadog and the gradient excess of Neon/Supabase (both negative anchors in the brief). The sensory anchor (cooling hum, dim red glow, quiet keyboard) translates to UI personality: subdued, purposeful, reverent toward data.

**Research basis:**
- Observatory lighting standards (Cademuir, 2013; NASA-STD-3001 Technical Brief) confirm red safelights as the professional standard for dark-adaptation preservation
- Planetarium design (Sherwood Observatory, 2026; Irene W. Pennington Planetarium) documents the emotional impact of emergence-from-darkness and the cognitive load of managing scale-invariant constellations
- NASA Artemis Mission Control (NASA 2025 flight control room inauguration) and Apollo 11 restoration (Wallpaper*, 2019) show the psychological power of purposeful, non-decorative alert coloring
- Linear design system research (How we redesigned the Linear UI, Linear 2025) confirms LCH color space adoption for perceptual uniformity and the rejection of HSL's arbitrary hue-saturation trades — supporting the choice of derived, real-world colors over generated palettes
- Raycast's minimal chrome philosophy (Raycast 2025 documentation) aligns the command-palette emergence metaphor with the planetarium's "silence until summoned" operational mode
