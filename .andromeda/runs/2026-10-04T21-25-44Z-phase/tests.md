## Patterns to follow
- **Re-exec child witness.** The parent re-runs the test binary with `--exact <fn>` and sets each child's environment, so env manipulation lives in the child, never in the parent test process. Per test-plan §3 Process-end witness form and the §4 corpus `race_free` precedent, which spawns re-exec children against a test-scoped entry and lock file.
- **Red before green, then a mutation check.** A behavioural pin is shown RED at the base, GREEN after the fix, and RED again when the fix is mutated out. Per test-plan §4 corpus crate, `race_free` was "RED on the prior unlocked code … mutation-checked". For the CARRY: a lock file is present after the skip arm at base and absent after the fix.
- **Clippy as an arm-wiring guard.** Clippy's lint verdict can stand as a guard in its own right, as in test-plan §4 interpretation crate (`cfg_attr(... allow(dead_code))`, with clippy as the Linux-arm guard). Here the analogue is clippy's `incompatible_msrv` against the raised `rust-version`. Its limit is that it covers APIs only (scope CONTEXT).
- **`app_info` contract assertion.** Per test-plan §5 Cross-module patterns → app_info contract, `rust_version` must be present in the response. Research must answer whether the existing fixture and assertion compare against a literal or against `CARGO_PKG_RUST_VERSION`.

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- NEVER call `std::env::set_var` mid-test without cleanup to reach the no-credential-store arm (per test-plan §11 Mocking). Put the env difference into a re-exec child's environment instead.
- NEVER land a floor witness, or a skip-arm leg, as a `#[ignore]` test without an open tracking issue (per test-plan §11 Quality).
- NEVER treat a nextest skip count, or a clean-skipping leg, as proof that the skip arm ran or left nothing behind (per test-plan §4 corpus crate, the `--success-output immediate` / `lacks [skip]` rule). A clean skip passes vacuously on a tree that never reaches the arm.
